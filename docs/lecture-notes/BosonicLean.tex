\vspace{-2em}
\noindent Gaussian formalism expresses properties of bosonic quantum states in terms
of phase-space data. For Gaussian states, the first moments and covariance matrix
determine the state: the former give its mean position and momentum, and the latter
describe its fluctuations. These notes develop the matrix algebra of covariances
and connect it with explicit normalized Gaussian wave functions, laying foundations
for the study of Gaussian matrix product states. For general background, see
Weedbrook et al.~\cite[Sec.~II]{weedbrook}. \vetb{Sections~\ref{sec:fock} and~\ref{sec:fockspace} construct the
formal Fock space of all coefficient families over the number basis, with its ladder operators and the Bargmann
picture, and place the bosonic Fock space inside it, where coefficient-finite maps define closed operators and
closed forms.} \vetd{Section~\ref{sec:secondquant} second-quantizes complex matrices on the formal Fock space, and
Section~\ref{sec:blochmessiah} factors every real symplectic matrix into orthogonal symplectic matrices and squeezing.}
\vete{Sections~\ref{sec:displacement} and~\ref{sec:gaussunitary} construct the displacement and squeezing operators and
the Gaussian unitaries on the bosonic Fock space, and Section~\ref{sec:puregaussian} characterizes the pure Gaussian
states by their characteristic functions.}

\section*{About these notes}
\noindent\revs{These notes are the mathematical account of the Lean library BosonicLean. We assume}
finite-dimensional linear algebra, complex inner products,
Lebesgue integration and elementary differential calculus\revs{; Section~\ref{sec:fock} needs only
algebra and formal power series}\vetb{, and Section~\ref{sec:fockspace} assumes the elements of closed operators
and forms on Hilbert space, as in Kato~\cite{kato}}\vetd{; Sections~\ref{sec:secondquant} and~\ref{sec:blochmessiah}
use the spectral theorem for normal and symmetric matrices, the positive square root, and, in Proposition~\ref{prop:sq-passive-exp}, the functional calculus of direct sums of self-adjoint operators}\vete{; Sections~\ref{sec:displacement}--\ref{sec:puregaussian}
use bounded and unitary operators on Hilbert space and the square root of a positive semidefinite matrix}\revs{. The reading guide below relates our conventions to those of the
literature, the roadmap after it shows which sections use which, and an index of terms precedes
the references.}
