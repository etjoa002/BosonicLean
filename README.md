# BosonicLean

The mathematics of bosonic quantum systems, formalized in Lean 4 over Mathlib: Gaussian covariance
matrices, the algebra of the canonical commutation relations (CCR) with its states and Gaussian
channels, the formal and the bosonic Fock space, Gaussian unitaries and pure Gaussian states.

Bosonic systems are those of light, of lattice vibrations and of cold atoms: each mode behaves like a
quantum harmonic oscillator. Lean is a computer program that checks every step of a proof, so a
result here can be trusted by reading its statement; the statements are collected on one page,
[`verify/CORE.md`](verify/CORE.md).

For `n` modes with coordinates `(q₁, …, qₙ, p₁, …, pₙ)`, a real symmetric matrix `γ` is the
covariance matrix of a quantum state exactly when it is **admissible** (the library proves that the
condition is necessary, and sufficient for a pure state when `γ` is also symplectic; sufficiency for
mixed states is among the planned results under Status),

```
γ − iΩ ≥ 0,      Ω = [[0, I], [−I, 0]],
```

with the normalization in which the vacuum has `γ = I`. Much of the library proves that the
operations which build Gaussian states preserve admissibility. Over a general real symplectic
space `(V, σ)` the theory is stated for the Weyl operators, `W(u) W(w) = e^{−iσ(u,w)/2} W(u+w)`,
and the C*-algebra they generate.

## What is proved

| | Main results | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Covariance matrices | Admissibility is preserved by symplectic congruence and restriction to subsystems; `γ₁ ⊕ γ₂` is admissible exactly when both summands are; admissible matrices are positive definite | §1 |
| Gaussian wave functions | The one-mode Gaussian is a normalized Schwartz function whose covariance is `diag(s², ħ²/s²)`; for `s² = ħ/(mω)` this is `diag(ħ/(mω), ħmω)` | §2 |
| Gaussian matrix product states | Contraction of virtual modes, as a Schur complement, preserves admissibility; periodic chains, their Fourier block diagonalization, product covariances, Gaussian kernels and the finitely squeezed cluster chain | §3–5 |
| The CCR algebra | Weyl operators on `ℓ²(V)` and the Weyl relation; Weyl systems and their pullback; relabelling by symplectic isomorphisms; for nondegenerate `σ` the algebra is simple, and its automorphisms of Weyl form are symplectic | |
| States and Gaussian states | States are determined by their characteristic functions `χ(u) = ω(W(u))`; the covariance form of a Gaussian state satisfies `γ + iσ ≥ 0` | |
| Gaussian channels | The transformation of Gaussian data under a channel, concatenation, the noiseless case and the dilation identities; for finitely many modes, channel matrices preserve admissibility | |
| The formal Fock space | Ladder operators with `[aᵢ, aⱼ†] = δᵢⱼ`; exponentials of operators that raise the particle number; the Bargmann map to formal power series, with `aⱼ† ↦ zⱼ` and `aⱼ ↦ ∂/∂zⱼ`; for one mode the anti-normal-ordered monomials `aᵐ(a†)ᵏ` span the algebra generated by `a` and `a†` | §6 |
| The bosonic Fock space | `Γ_s` is `ℓ²` of the occupation numbers inside the formal Fock space; the maximal realization of a coefficient-finite operator is closed, and so is the form of finitely many such operators | §7 |
| Second quantization | `Γ(g)` as the substitution `z ↦ gᵀz` in the Bargmann picture; `Γ(gg') = Γ(g)Γ(g')` and the intertwining with the ladder operators; for unitary `𝒰`, `Γ(𝒰)` is a strongly continuous unitary representation; `Γ(eʰ) = e^{dΓ(h)}` coefficientwise | §8 |
| The Bloch–Messiah decomposition | Every real symplectic matrix is `O_𝒰 Σ(r) O_𝒱` with `r ≥ 0`, unique up to order; the polar decomposition of symplectic matrices | §9 |
| Displacement and squeezing | Exponential vectors are total; displacement and squeezing operators are unitary; the Weyl relation, `D(α)* a D(α) = a + α`, `S(ζ) D(β) = D(L_ζ β) S(ζ)`, strong continuity; irreducibility of the displacements | §10 |
| Gaussian unitaries | Gaussian unitaries in Weyl form, their composition and the uniqueness of their data; displacements, passive unitaries and real squeezers are Gaussian; the factorization `U = λ Γ(𝒰) D(α) S(r) Γ(𝒱)` (Tjoa–Cirac, Proposition 5); `U* R̂ U = M R̂ + d` on the common domain of the annihilation operators | §11 |
| Pure Gaussian states | A vector is determined up to a phase by its characteristic function; `ψ = U|0⟩` for a Gaussian unitary `U` exactly when `ψ` has a Gaussian characteristic function, whose covariance matrix is admissible and symplectic, and every admissible symplectic matrix occurs, with any displacement vector; pure Gaussian states have a nonzero vacuum component, so number states `|m⟩`, `m ≠ 0`, are not Gaussian | §12 |
| Quadratic Hamiltonians | For `H = ½ R̂ᵀKR̂ + fᵀR̂` with `K` real symmetric and `f` real, `H` is essentially self-adjoint on the finitely supported vectors, its closure generates exactly one unitary group `e^{-itH}`, and `e^{-itH}` is a Gaussian unitary whose data are the classical flow of `½ xᵀKx + fᵀx`; the construction uses analytic vectors and no spectral theorem | §13 |

The precise statements are the 115 theorems of [`verify/CORE.md`](verify/CORE.md), under headings
that refine the rows of this table; the core states the results on quadratic Hamiltonians as well.
The condition `γ + iσ ≥ 0` is proved necessary for a Gaussian
state; a Gaussian channel is a completely positive map by definition. Conventions are listed in
`CONVENTIONS.md`.

## Verification

The 115 theorems of the core are the main results, the statements that fix the library's
definitions in Mathlib's terms (31 entries, listed at the head of `verify/Challenge.lean`; some of
them are also main results, and the page marks the defining parts "(definition)"), and 14 examples
(the vacuum, a squeezed covariance, the attenuator channel, a state of the CCR algebra, a coherent
state, a squeezed vacuum, and others) in which the hypotheses hold and the objects are not
degenerate. `verify/Challenge.lean` states them without proofs, and
`verify/Solution.lean` proves them from the library. The command

```
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid
```

checks that every proved theorem has exactly the stated form, that no axioms beyond `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` are used, and that Lean's kernel and four independently
written proof checkers accept every proof. A reader who accepts the 115 statements therefore need
not read any proof. Besides the definitions described in the core, two properties of the CCR
algebra are taken from the library: it is a star-ordered ring, and it is not the zero algebra. The
displacement and squeezing operators and the unitaries of second quantization are, by their type,
elements of Mathlib's unitary group of the bounded operators; the core uses this and states their
action. The same check is set up to run on GitHub at every change.

`lake comparator` is the proof judge maintained by the Lean developers for proofs whose author is
not trusted, including proofs produced by AI; see
[leanprover/comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator) and the chapter
[Validating a Lean Proof](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/#validating-comparator)
of the Lean reference manual. Among its checkers are
[lean4lean](https://github.com/digama0/lean4lean) and [nanoda](https://github.com/ammkrn/nanoda_lib).
On Linux it isolates the compiled code in a sandbox; elsewhere it needs the option
`--inadvisably-no-sandbox`.

## Build and verify

With the Lean installer `elan`:

```
git clone https://github.com/etjoa002/BosonicLean
cd BosonicLean
lake exe cache get          # download the precompiled Mathlib
lake build BosonicLean
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid
```

The library depends only on [Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4); the
versions of Lean and Mathlib are fixed in `lean-toolchain` and `lake-manifest.json`, and the
libraries built on this one fix the same versions.

## Methods of proof

Each operation on Gaussian states acts on the admissibility matrix by a congruence, a direct sum
or a Schur complement, all of which preserve positivity; the contraction of virtual modes,
physically a projection onto EPR pairs, is a Schur complement. The CCR algebra is realized on
`ℓ²(V)` with `V` discrete, where Weyl operators are phase-weighted translations. On the formal
Fock space every coefficient of an exponential is a finite sum, so no question of convergence
arises.

## The notes

[`docs/lecture-notes/BosonicLean.pdf`](docs/lecture-notes/BosonicLean.pdf) gives the account with
proofs, examples and a dictionary to the physics literature, in the sections listed in the table
above. A tag beside a theorem links to the Lean declarations that prove it. One remark in the
notes on pure Gaussian states says that we have not checked whether the formula for the minorant
γ₀ = γ^{1/2}|A|γ^{1/2} appears in the literature; the formula itself is proved.

## Library layout

`PhaseSpace/` covariance matrices; `Quantum/` one-mode wave functions; `Analysis/` supporting
analysis; `LinearAlgebra/` supporting linear algebra; `CStar/` C*-algebra facts; `Weyl/` the CCR
algebra, its states and channels, Gaussian unitaries, pure Gaussian states and quadratic
Hamiltonians; `GaussianTNLean/` Gaussian matrix product states; `Fock/` the formal and the bosonic
Fock space, second quantization, displacement and squeezing; `Examples/` two examples that use the library through its root file; `verify/` the core.

## Status

The library is under development, and this repository is updated as the work progresses; see
`CONTRIBUTING.md` for problem reports and suggestions. Planned next are notes for the CCR algebra
and its states and channels, two-dimensional Gaussian tensor networks, and the connection between
covariance matrices and the formal Fock space. Candidates for later theorems: a Gaussian state for
every covariance form satisfying `γ + iσ ≥ 0`, on the CCR algebra and, for finitely many modes,
for mixed states; complete positivity of every map whose data satisfy
the channel condition (Demoen–Vanheuverzwijn–Verbeure); exponentials of operators that lower the
particle number, such as displacement and squeezing; the Choi representation of Gaussian channels.
